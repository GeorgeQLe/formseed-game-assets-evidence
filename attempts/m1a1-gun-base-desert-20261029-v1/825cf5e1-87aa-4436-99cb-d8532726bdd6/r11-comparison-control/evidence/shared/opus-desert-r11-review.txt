# Abrams gun base: r11 vs r10, checked against the two source photos

**Bottom line:** r11 removes every cheek-strip artifact I could see in r10. I found no new defect at this sheet's resolution. The roof-height, angled, non-projecting gun base still reads correctly and still matches the sources. One small source-fit gap carries over unchanged from r10. This is not an approval.

## r10 → r11 differences
I read both PNGs at full sheet resolution and couldn't crop or zoom. The tank-scale panels in rows 1–2 look the same in both. Every change is on the cheek and gun-base edges:

| Close-up panel | r10 | r11 |
|---|---|---|
| Front three-quarter low | Small light/dark spur where the image-left cheek's top meets its front edge | Clean edge with a continuous highlight line |
| Rear three-quarter | Small dark sliver at the cheek's front-top corner, above the mantlet | Clean; the highlight strip follows the cheek top |
| Front | Thin dark sliver on the right of the mantlet's top edge | Gone |
| Top | Two dark spike notches along the right cheek's inner edge | Gone; both cheek inner edges are straight dark strips |

These match what you described: the self-crossing on the left cheek strip is gone, and only the Y positions of the front cheek vertices changed. The cheek outlines, the mantlet frame, the roof trapezoid and the cheek wedges look the same as r10 in every panel. I don't see any shape changing that shouldn't have.

## Fit to the source photos
- **Roof height, no projecting box:** this holds. In the 2003 Houghton front photo, the mantlet is a roughly square slab set back between the angled cheeks, with its top close to the cheek and roof line. The r11 front close-up shows the same: a square frame (about 165×160 px) set back between the cheeks, with nothing sticking out past them. The 1986 Fort Knox three-quarter view shows a similar slot between the cheeks, and the front three-quarter low close-up matches it.
- **Angled cheeks:** the shape and angle are consistent with both photos.
- **Port position:** the small port at the mantlet's upper left matches the port in the Houghton photo.
- **Gap (carried over from r10):** the top of the mantlet frame sits a little below the roof edge (about 35 px in the close-up). In the Houghton photo it is nearly flush. This is a small proportion difference, not a regression.

## Camera blocking vs modelled shape vs source limits
- **Camera blocking, not shape:** in the physical-left and physical-right close-ups, the smoke-grenade launchers and the gun barrel cover the gun base, so those panels say almost nothing about it. They look the same in r10 and r11.
- **Lighting, not shape:** the diagonal dark band across the front views is the barrel's cast shadow.
- **Source limits:**
  - The Fort Knox photo is black-and-white and taken from high above at three-quarter angle.
  - The Houghton photo is head-on, so it shows little depth.
  - Neither photo shows the gun base from directly above, so how deep the mantlet is set back is only loosely constrained by the references.
  - Stowage, crew and camouflage in the photos hide some edges.

## What I didn't check
- I didn't re-check any of your GLB numbers: the self-crossing count, face directions, notch hit tests, open edges, the 1.81 mm projection, or the 96 pose/recoil pairs. I only compared images.
- Artifacts smaller than about one pixel at this sheet scale can't be ruled out.
- As you noted, the 96 coarse pose samples don't cover continuous motion or the animation.

This is image evidence only. It doesn't amount to a structured evaluation, an adversarial review, or human approval.
