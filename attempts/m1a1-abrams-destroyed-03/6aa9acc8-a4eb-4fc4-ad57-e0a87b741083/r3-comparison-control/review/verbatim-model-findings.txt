### Analogue check (`usmc-hansen.jpg`, as a broad visual analogue only)
The photo shows blackening and paint loss across the whole turret and hull, and an ash bed at the base. v85 shows none of that: outside a roughly one-panel radius the tank is untouched tan. The halo deals only with the local rim.

### Strongest objections
1. **The tank doesn't read as destroyed.** In `1000-front-three-quarter.png`, `1000-rear-three-quarter.png` and every 256 and 128 view, it is a clean tan tank with one dark roof opening. That remains a condition gap, broader than this finish.
2. **The halo is too soft.** Its even, airbrushed falloff in `1000-top.png` could pass for a paint vignette, and it adds no fractured texture.
3. **The rupture still has no violent shape.** The straight, crisp rim and the lack of a visible broken panel, both raised in the prior W03 v72 review, are unchanged. A finish can't supply those.
4. **The finish is invisible at 128 and marginal at 256.**
5. **Recognition is intact.** I have no objection to its primary forms or materials.

### Recommendation: **KEEP THIS FINISH DIRECTION WITH LIMITS**
The local halo is a sound layer. In `1000-top.png` and `1000-front-three-quarter.png` it moves the cavity slightly toward reading as damage rather than an opened hatch. Limits:
- On its own it doesn't make the tank read as destroyed.
- Its value is limited to 1000 and the top view.
- It must not stand in for whole-vehicle condition work or for fracture geometry at the rim.

**The physical direction (the cavity) is still useful.** The prior W03 v72 physical findings stand, and no approval transfers.
