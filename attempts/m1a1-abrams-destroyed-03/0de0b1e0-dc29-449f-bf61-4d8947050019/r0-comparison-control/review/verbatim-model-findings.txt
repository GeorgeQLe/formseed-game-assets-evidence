## W03: destroyed03-torn-final-v106 (job 0de0b1e0…, r0, CP 616092aa…)

**What I inspected**
- **v106:** all six 256 views; 1000 top, rear three-quarter and left; 128 top and rear three-quarter.
- **v101:** 1000 top and rear three-quarter.
- **Clean:** 1000 rear three-quarter.
- **Reference:** the Hansen photo, used only as a broad analogue.

**What I observed**
- **The panel finish change is the dominant visible change, not the geometry.**
  - In `1000-top`, both bustle-roof panels are now pale cream like clean: the left one at x≈410–490 and the right one around the opening. In v101 both were brown.
  - The soot now surrounds two clean-looking plates. The burn stops sharply at their rectangular borders, and the left plate keeps its dark outline.
  - `1000-rear-three-quarter` (x≈525–760, y≈545–620) shows the same thing: a pale square with a dark opening beside a pale closed square. That reads more strongly as twin service hatches, one open, than v101 did.
- **The rim is still essentially rectangular.**
  - In `1000-top` the cavity (x≈515–585, y≈232–312) is a quadrilateral with one clipped corner and slight skew.
  - In the rear three-quarter view, a faint raised lip shows on its left edge. In `1000-left`, a small pale lip pokes above the bustle roofline (x≈680–760, y≈487–495).
  - The four displaced rim stations do not read as torn or petalled metal at 1000. At 256 (`256-top`, `256-rear-three-quarter`) the opening reads as an open square hatch.
- **The fragment does not read as a fragment.**
  - The grounded piece is a flat, dark, irregular polygon: x≈675–715, y≈185–255 in `1000-top`, and x≈470–540, y≈855–890 in the rear three-quarter view.
  - It looks grounded and is not floating. But it reads as a scorch, oil stain or dark cut-out shape, not as a metal plate thrown from the roof.
  - It has no thickness or pale torn edge, and it does not match the opening's size or tone. In `256-front` it is a dark sliver on the ground (x≈230–245, y≈240).
- **Whole form.**
  - `256-right`, `256-left`, `256-front` and `256-front-three-quarter` are effectively the clean tank, except for the small ground sliver in front.
  - At 128 the top and rear three-quarter views show only a small dark roof patch.
  - I am not counting the rear damage being hidden in the front views against it, as instructed. In the views where it is exposed, though, it still does not read as destroyed.
  - Compared with the Hansen photo's broad morphology (overall char, broken-up camouflage, deformed and scattered metal, ash on the ground), this is a clean tank with one roof hole.

**Strongest remaining objections**
1. **The rim does not read as fracture.** The physical rim edit is too subtle to overcome the rectangular service-opening reading at 1000 and 256. This is unchanged in substance from the v72/v85/v91/v101 rim findings.
2. **The fallback panels create a serious ambiguity.** Clean pale plates inside a soot field read as intact or replaced hatch lids, actively reinforcing "deliberate opening". I can't judge the physical edit apart from this, because the finish defect dominates the read.
3. **The fragment reads as a stain or prop, not as torn armor.**
4. **Whole-vehicle condition gap.** This persists from v101.

**Recommendation: REJECT the physical direction as executed.** The cavity and the "something ejected to the ground" idea are coherent concepts worth keeping in a successor. But the four-station rim displacement and the flat proxy fragment don't move the read from service hatch to fracture. And the untextured panel fallback makes the hatch reading stronger, not weaker. A successor would need much larger visible rim deformation, a fragment with plate thickness and a torn edge, and restored panel soot before further finishing could be judged.
