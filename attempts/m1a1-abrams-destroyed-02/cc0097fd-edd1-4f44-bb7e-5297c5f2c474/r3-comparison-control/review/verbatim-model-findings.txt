## W02 — destroyed02-rearburn-v78 (job `cc0097fd…` r3, checkpoint `c9277f5a…`, GLB `106b94a0…`)

**Context.** Five source-bound atlases cover Hull, RearExhaustGrilles, RearGrilleRemnant, LeftFender and RightFender. The pre-author audit predicted a "broad near-uniform charcoal plateau… can still read as a painted rectangle/airbrushed fade and may not show ash." Separately, the actual bounds reach max z 8.215 against the fixed framing's 7.925, so a broken vane protrudes. It is visible but not cropped in the right, left and top views (top: thin sliver at x≈545–595, y≈20–50).

### Six 256 views
- **`256-front-three-quarter.png`**: Clean tan, with nothing visible from the front. The rear fire is fully hidden by normal camera occlusion.
- **`256-rear-three-quarter.png`**: A dark brown rear plate and rear deck edge. It is clearly darker than v68, but the cavity becomes a dark spot inside a dark plate.
- **`256-front.png`**: Clean, no damage cues.
- **`256-left.png`**: Darkening at the rear end plus the protruding vane, and nothing else.
- **`256-right.png`**: The same rear-end darkening as on the left side.
- **`256-top.png`**: A dark band across the full width of the rear deck end, with darker rear fender tips on both sides.

### 1000/128 comparison with v68, v54 and clean (same cameras)
- **`1000-rear-three-quarter.png`**: v54 (physical) and v68 both show the broken-vane cavity on a tan plate, with v68 adding a faint sepia wash. v78 turns the whole rear plate and the back strip of the deck dark brown (x≈620–920, y≈670–830), with a diagonal soft fade into the tan. The value change is real and stronger than v68's weak airbrush. However:
  - it is a smooth, uniform plateau with no ash, pale or white tones and no mottling;
  - the fade edge is a clean linear ramp.
  
  It reads as soot spray or a dark-painted tail, not burned paint.
- **Cavity**: At 1000 the bent vanes and dark interior (x≈705–820, y≈735–805) still read as a genuinely broken opening rather than a service panel, because of the splayed vane fragments. But the dark plate **reduces contrast** with the hole. It was more legible on tan in v54 and v68. At 256 it is marginal, and at 128 it is gone.
- **`1000-right.png`** vs v68 and v54: A horizontal fade from near-black at the rear (x≈50) to tan by about x≈130 on the upper rear hull, plus a solid black lower rear corner (x≈50–90, y≈585–645). v68 had a brown-grey fade over a similar span. v78 is darker but has the same airbrushed shape.
- **`1000-left.png`**: Nearly a mirror of the right view (x≈870–950). The darkening is **symmetric** from both sides and in `1000-top.png`, which weakens the designed asymmetry. In practice it reads as a uniformly darkened tail.
- **`1000-top.png`** vs v68: v68 had a light sepia rear band. v78 has a darker brown rectangle across the full deck width (x≈370–630, y≈50–110) with straight lateral edges matching the hull outline. It reads as a **large rectangle with a fade**, not an irregular thermal field.
- **`128-rear-three-quarter/right/top.png`**: A slightly darker tail on a clean tank. I would not recognise it as destroyed.
- **Disclosed underbody seam (0.09254)**: Not observed. The underbody is effectively hidden from all six cameras, so this is unproven either way, not a finding.
- **Camouflage or new seam**: Not camouflage, since there is a single blob. I saw no new hard seam beyond the straight hull-outline edges of the plateau.

### Strongest objections
1. **It is still airbrush, now darker (core material).** The plateau and linear fade in `1000-rear-three-quarter.png`, `1000-right.png` and `1000-top.png` read as dark paint or a soot spray. The "restrained ash" is not visible in any view.
2. **Destroyed recognition (core).** Seen as a whole, about 85–90% of the vehicle is clean tan, and the front three views show nothing. A rear-only fire can be legitimate, but the rear evidence alone has to carry "destroyed," and at 256 and 128 it doesn't.
3. **The finish hurts the physical damage.** The cavity, the strongest real damage cue, loses contrast against the darkened plate (`256-rear-three-quarter.png` vs `destroyed02-finish-v68/256-rear-three-quarter.png`).
4. **Symmetry and rectangle.** Left and right are mirror images and the top shows a full-width straight band, so the designed irregular field does not come through.

### Recommendation: **REJECT THIS FINISH**
It improves on v68's value strength but not its kind: it is still a smooth airbrush, now darker and rectangular. Keep the v54 physical rear cavity and broken vanes as the precursor; that geometry still reads as genuine breakage at 1000. A successor finish would need:
- broken, mottled char with pale ash in the tones, not a uniform plateau;
- a field that breaks the deck-width rectangle and the left/right symmetry;
- a lighter rim or heat halo so the cavity separates from the plate instead of sinking into it.

v68 and all earlier failures stay charged, and v78 is added to that ledger. The Hansen packet is not treated as a rear-incident or temperature-map source.
