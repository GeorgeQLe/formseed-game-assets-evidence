# W01 M1A1 destroyed-01 whole-body burnout v89: read-only actual PBR review

**Bottom line:** v89 is not yet usable as a preliminary burned-out tank. It is still clearly an Abrams at every size. It has two unresolved primary defects:

- **Burnout read:** from the three-quarter views and from the left it does not read as burned out. At 128 and 256 it reads as an intact tank in a plain gray paint scheme.
- **Running gear:** the right running gear reads as wheels standing on chocks, not on track.

The source checks and the visual review are separate. The source validation passed. The independent native/mask audit may still be pending, which is not a failure. Nothing here implies human approval, a score, a formal go, or a reset of the attempt history.

- **Material:** the matte gray (roughness 0.86, no metal anywhere) reads as primer or a gray paint scheme.
  - Compared with S/usmc-hansen.jpg, which I am using only as a broad cross-period analogue, not an exact incident or pattern:

## Strongest objections
1. **Burnout is not legible from the three-quarter views, the left side, or at 128 and 256.** Desaturating the whole body has swapped "pristine tan" for "pristine gray". This is a primary finish defect.
2. **Clean tan accessories and clear blue glass contradict a burnout** and draw the eye at every size.
3. **The ash blobs read as camouflage or smoke graphics:** soft-edged, near-white clouds. The only convincing soot is around the hatches, and it is one-sided.
4. **The running gear reads as chocks** (detailed below). A passing material check does not excuse this physical objection.

## Running gear
- In `1000-front-three-quarter.png`, the seven dark pads sit one under each wheel, all the same size and evenly spaced:
  - roughly x≈150–185 / y≈630 through x≈420–455 / y≈795;
  - with open ground between them;
  - with nothing linking them along the run.
- They read as wheel chocks or pedestals. The tank looks jacked up on blocks, not resting on short lengths of track.
- The gray body makes them stand out more than the tan body of v62 and v77 did; the geometry and placement look identical across all three versions.
- The fanned shoe group at the rear (x≈70–140, y≈575–615) and the flat stack at the bow (x≈455–545, y≈795–845) still look like laid-out planks or spare shoes.
- At 256 they show as dark ticks under each wheel. At 128 they are not readable.
- I make no claim about what caused the failure, about mobility, or about engineering. This is purely what the image shows.

## Recommendations
- **Finish: KEEP DIRECTION WITH LIMITS.**
  - Covering the whole body is right, and it fixes v77's main failure of leaving 75–80% of the tank clean.
  - Limit 1: flat desaturation on its own fails as the burnout carrier, so v89 itself does not pass as a burnout read.
  - Limit 2: a passing finish needs strong light-and-dark variation (soot-dark, ash-pale) on every side, including the left and the top.
  - Limit 3: the remaining tan casings and accessories need to stop reading as factory-fresh.
  - Limit 4: the ash shapes need to stop reading as clouds.
- **Physical running gear: REJECT DIRECTION.**
  - Isolated, evenly spaced one-per-wheel pads read as chocks in the three-quarter view, and they have done so through v62, v77 and v89.
  - What is still open is the look of track: linked shoes running under several wheels, with breaks that don't line up with each wheel.
  - The v62 escalation condition, which calls for reopening the wheel-station geometry, still stands.

**Is the whole model usable as a preliminary burned-out tank?** No. Recognition is fine, but the finish legibility and the chock-like supports are both unresolved primary defects. v89 counts against the attempt budget, all earlier attempts stay counted, and nothing is reset.
