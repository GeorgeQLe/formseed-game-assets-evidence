## W01: destroyed01-charcoal-v102 (job a073f448… r2, checkpoint 37466d1d…)

**Recommendation: KEEP the direction for more finishing, with limitations.** The gray camouflage objection is resolved for the base color but not for the pattern.

**Views I read:** all six at 256px; at 1000px the front-three-quarter, front, left, right, top and rear-three-quarter; at 128px the front-three-quarter, left, top and rear-three-quarter. I compared against v96 at 1000px (front-three-quarter and top) and v94 at 1000px (left).

**Is the gray camouflage objection resolved?**
- **Base color: yes.** v96 was a cool mid-gray. v102 is a warm near-black charcoal-brown across the hull, turret, gun, skirts and bustle. The darkest tones are close to black, which v96 never reached.
- **Running gear: yes.** v96's pale wheels, hubs, bow guards and sprocket are now dark. The 128px left view no longer shows a row of bright wheel dots.
- **Pattern: not resolved.**
  - The pale marks now look like an even, fine-grained mottle spread over every face, like leopard-spot camouflage or frost.
  - Clearest examples in 1000-front-three-quarter: the turret cheek around x≈420–640, y≈460–600, and the glacis around x≈640–820, y≈560–700.
  - In 1000-front, the patches on the turret face (around x≈360–420 and x≈690–760, y≈520–620) read as bright splash camouflage.
  - The marks are the same size everywhere and are not concentrated around openings, so they read as a designed paint scheme rather than heat damage.
  - In the Hansen photo, by contrast, the pale areas are large and irregular: ash-white skirt panels, rust-brown areas, and the original camouflage partly burnt through, with very different results on different faces.

**Remaining core objections (finish):**
1. **Mottle reads as camouflage or snow, not heat.** Stroke scale and density are even across the hull, turret, glacis and top. There is no localised scorching, and v89's soot around the hatches is still missing.
2. **Too dark and too uniform.**
   - Nothing on the vehicle is rust-brown, ash-white or bare metal. Every face is close to the same near-black, so at 128px it reads as a black or dark-painted tank.
   - That is a big improvement on the gray tank, but it is not yet clearly burnt out. The Hansen photo, which I treat only as a broad analogue, shows mostly mid-tones with strong contrast between faces.
3. **Clean parts remain:**
   - tan sight boxes and the commander's sight, clearly visible in 1000-front at x≈405–445 and x≈640–675;
   - tan bases on the smoke-launcher mounts;
   - pale gray machine guns and mounts;
   - clear blue vision-block glass;
   - pale gray rear rail and grille.
   
   These still look intact and freshly painted on a burnt hull.
4. **Artifacts:** I saw no hard UV split. The mantlet block in 1000-front still reads as a separately toned panel.

**Physical damage:**
- The continuous steel track is retained from v94: seated wheels, no hovering or floating parts, a symmetric front view and a coherent loop.
- Like the Hansen photo, the vehicle has no skirts, so the road wheels are exposed. That works for a burnout read.
- The track and running gear still look intact and able to run. There is no visible track or running-gear damage. That remains an open question of scope, not a rendering failure.

**Readability and recognition:**
- It is recognisable as an M1A1 in every view and size.
- At 128px it reads as "dark tank". That is better than v96's "gray tank", but the mottle disappears at that size and nothing says "burnt".
- Pending native or mask work is not counted against it.

## Shared caveats

- **Reference photo.** I used the Hansen photo only as a broad analogue of a burned 2003 USMC M1A1: large irregular tonal breakup, a rust/ash/char range, partly surviving camouflage and ash on the ground. It is not a template for paint geometry or for the exact incident.
- **Appearance only.** Both candidates are designed adaptations. Neither reproduces the photo. My findings are about appearance, not about whether the vehicle could run, what happened to it, or what caused the damage.
- **No approval implied.** Technical validation passing is not a visual go. These are informal directions from one reviewer: no scores, no claimed consensus, no approval, no human decision. I modified no files.
