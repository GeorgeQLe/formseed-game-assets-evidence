**Verdict: REJECT the current v110 finish.** This rejects only the finish on top of the source, not the v108 physical form. It is not a score, a formal go, a consensus or human approval. I modified no files.

**What I looked at:** in `destroyed01-regional-v110/`, all six 1000 px views, all six 256 views, and the 128 px left and front-three-quarter views. I compared them with the same fixed cameras in v108 (left, right, front), v96 (left), v102 (left) and clean-desert-v1-r2 (left, front-three-quarter). I also looked at the two reference photos, treated only as broad wreck analogues. The folder has no `render-evidence.json` or overview sheets at the expected names. That means I could not check the image hashes against job 80820fc0 r5 / checkpoint 585f21f6…, and nothing here can confirm the source identity.

## Neutral observations

**Is it an Abrams?** Yes, clearly at 1000 and 256. At 128, the left view still reads as an Abrams. The 128 front-three-quarter view reads as a dark tank but loses most interior detail.

**Fallen/settled form:** the hull sits low on a flat, straight track strip in both side views. In the front view, the black lower hull block plus the narrow dark wheel columns still look like "stilts." The new tan-brown fender tops over black columns make that worse than in v108. The raised front idler and rear sprocket sit above the end of the flat track, and no track wraps around them.

**Burn finish – coverage and placement:**
- About 85–90% of the surfaces are one even dark chocolate brown. The fender tops and lit turret faces show as a lighter tan-brown, but that comes from lighting, not from any burn pattern.
- The only real coating-loss/ash marks are the pale grey-white blotches on the **left** side skirts. There is about one blotch per panel, all at similar heights and sizes. That steady repeat reads as a **camouflage or stripe pattern**, not as fire damage.
- On the right side, the matching marks are faint brown/grey smudges that hardly show. The top view has a few pale smudges near the hatches and nothing else.
- There is no clear black char, no ash run-off at panel edges, no orange rust, and no leftover patches of the original paint.

**Burned-out tank or painted tank?** In the right, front, front-three-quarter, rear-three-quarter and top views, at all three sizes, it reads as a **brown-painted tank**. Only the left side reads partly as damage, and even there it looks like camouflage. The reference photos show the opposite structure: very uneven patches of black char, white/grey ash where the paint burned off, and surviving paint islands, especially on the turret and the skirt edges. v102 (charcoal) looked more burnt than v110. v110 is warmer and more even, and so reads more like paint.

**Accessories and running gear:**
- The **sight housings** (the boxes beside the commander's and loader's stations, and the front sight boxes) and the **ammunition box coatings** still show clean, sharp-edged desert tan. They match the clean asset, so they read as untouched.
- The **blue optic glass** (gunner's sight window, front and side vision blocks) is bright and saturated. It reads as preserved and unsooted, which clashes with a burnout.
- The M2 and M240 guns, the smoke-grenade launchers and the bustle rack are clean grey metal.
- The sprocket teeth and the track links are clean, even grey. The road wheels are darkened.

## After weighing the established evidence

- The Army source describes the final drive feeding from the hull to the sprocket. You also report that independent geometry shows four end-wheel hub-to-hull supports with positive overlap. So the claim that the raised end wheels are **floating or unsupported is false**, and I am not making it. Raised end gear does not need to touch the ground.
- What remains is a **visual** ambiguity about the load path and the track wrap. With no track wrapping the idler and sprocket, and with the clean grey teeth, the ends read as wheels hovering over a cut-short track, and the front still reads as stilts. That is a question about how the form reads, not a mechanical defect. A finish could ease it (dark, ash-covered end gear and hub housings) without any change to geometry.
- The static broken track ends need no cause and should not be given one. I judged condition only where it is visible; no label recognition is needed in hidden views.

## Objections to fix in a v110 successor

1. Swap the even brown overall finish for an uneven layout of char, ash and remaining paint. Paint loss should cover clearly visible areas on the turret sides, the turret top and the right-side skirts, not only the left skirts.
2. Break up the left-skirt blotch rhythm so the patches vary in size, height and edge shape and stop reading as camouflage.
3. Give the **right side** the same strength of burn as the left; at present its marks hardly show.
4. Soot, darken or partly burn the paint on the tan sight housings and ammunition boxes.
5. Dull or soot the blue optic glass.
6. Add char/heat staining to the grey guns, smoke launchers and bustle rack.
7. Darken and ash the sprocket teeth, the idler and the track. That should also soften the visual load-path and stilts problem at the end gear and in the front view.
8. Recheck the burn read at 128, where the even brown currently takes over completely.

Source mechanics, source geometry proof and producer technical validation are separate gates, and I did not judge them from these PNGs.
